Morning light reached the village before the first train arrived. Along the river, a narrow path curved between gardens and stone walls. Clara followed it with a notebook tucked beneath her arm, listening to birds announce the changing season. She had promised herself one quiet hour before opening the small bookshop near the square.

At the bridge, she stopped to watch leaves drift across the water. Each leaf turned slowly, caught a little sunlight, and disappeared beneath the wooden crossing. Nothing hurried there. Even the wind seemed willing to pause among the reeds and let the morning find its own rhythm.

A gardener waved from the opposite bank. Clara returned the greeting, then opened her notebook and wrote a sentence about the river. She crossed out two words, smiled, and tried again. The second sentence felt clearer. Sometimes a useful thought needed less decoration and more room to breathe.

When the clock above the station struck eight, she closed the notebook and walked toward town. Soon the shop would fill with questions, footsteps, and stories carried through the door. For now, she kept the river in mind and enjoyed the steady sound of her shoes against the quiet street ahead.
